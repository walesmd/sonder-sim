# The Event Log

*Reading-level experiment · target: elementary school · rewritten from `docs/posts/0002-the-event-log.md` · original untouched*

---

We are building pretend universes inside a computer. Ours starts from a magic number called a seed. Same seed, same universe, every single time. This universe's seed is 1893.

Nothing really lives in it yet. Two little stand-ins take turns. A pretend market makes prices wobble up and down. A pretend war office gathers soldiers. They are placeholders. Real decisions come later.

Here is what changed this time.

Before, when something happened, the computer printed it on the screen and forgot it. Like saying a sentence out loud with nobody writing it down. Close the program, and it was as if nothing had ever happened.

Now the universe keeps a diary. We call it the annals. Every single thing that happens gets written in it, one line at a time, in order. That is the new rule: nothing happens unless it goes in the book. And the words you see on screen? That is not the universe talking. That is a reader, reading the book out loud afterward. You could delete the reader completely. The universe would still write the exact same book, silently.

**Every entry must say what caused it.**

This is our favorite part. Each entry points back at an older entry. Prices moved because of where they were before. Soldiers gathered because of the last gathering. Follow the arrows back, and back, and back. You always land on page one: "a universe begins." That is the only entry allowed to have no cause.

So you can point at anything in the whole book and ask "why did that happen?" The book walks you down the chain, one link at a time, all the way to the start. Forever, for every universe we ever make.

One small rule makes this safe. An entry can only point backwards. You cannot blame something that has not been written yet. That means the chain can never go in a circle. No entry can be its own grandparent.

**Strict writer, gentle reader.**

We made two opposite rules on purpose.

Writing in the book is strict. You can never erase a page. A wrong page would be wrong forever. So the writer checks everything, and if anything is off, it refuses and stops right away. Better to stop now than to ruin history.

Reading the book is gentle. Someday an old reader will pick up a new book with words it has never seen. It should not panic. It just describes what it can see and moves on. Old readers meet new books. That is fine.

Banks have worked this way for hundreds of years. A bank does not just remember your money total. It writes down every deposit and every payment. Your total is what you get by adding the list up. The list is the truth.

**What we got wrong.**

Two honest confessions. First, while explaining all this, Claude wrote a sentence that was exactly backwards, and Mike got confused. That confusion was a bug. If an explanation confuses the person in charge, you fix the explanation or the idea. Second, every entry gets a size number, and the birth of the universe got size zero. The biggest event ever, with the smallest number! We have no size scale yet, so zero was the honest choice. It looks silly. We wrote it down anyway.

**Next.**

Right now the book lives only in the computer's short-term memory. Close the program, and the book vanishes. Strange, for a thing we keep calling the permanent record. Next time, we give it a real home on disk, so it can last.
