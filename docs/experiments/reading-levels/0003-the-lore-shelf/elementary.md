# The Lore Shelf

*Reading-level experiment · target: elementary school · rewritten from `docs/posts/0003-the-lore-shelf.md` · original untouched*

---

This time we wrote no computer code at all. We wrote stories about aliens instead. That might sound like a day off. It wasn't. The stories are tools, and this post explains how.

## The project, in one breath

Sonder is a pretend universe that runs inside a computer. Someday it will be full of alien civilizations. They will trade, argue, make friends, and make enemies. Right now it is almost empty. One make-believe market drifts around. One war office plans battles with no soldiers. That's it.

## Three aliens before breakfast

It started as a bedtime talk. Mike asked seven questions about any alien people. What is their home world like? How do they trade? What do they believe in? What dangers do they know about?

We answered the questions three times. Three alien civilizations came out.

- **The Vess** are living crystal. Their minds are made of many parts humming together. To them, trading with a stranger is a holy act.
- **The Continuance** is a machine mind. Its makers flew away and never came back. It waited so long, and planned so hard, that it slowly became a whole people.
- **The Marrow Fleet** are families who live on giant grown ships. They never land. They own almost nothing. They carry every rumor across the dark, and the rumors change a little on the way.

One Fleet story says some of their people stayed behind on the old home world. The Fleet never goes back to check on them. Going back might mean finding out everyone died. Or it might mean finding answers. Some mysteries hold a people together.

We picked three very different peoples on purpose. One went from many minds to one. One went from one mind to many. One stayed many, and stayed moving. We call this pile of stories the lore shelf.

## The big idea: stories as tests

Here is the sentence that took us three tries to get right. The stories are tests, not orders.

Think about sewing a backpack. Before you start, pick the heaviest things it must carry. A big book. A ball. A full water bottle. When the backpack is done, try to fit them in. If they don't fit, you fix the backpack. You never shrink the book.

Our alien stories are the big book and the ball. Every machine we build for Sonder must be able to hold them. If a machine can't hold the Vess, we fix the machine. Never the story.

But passing the test is only the start. A spelling list has ten words on it. That doesn't mean you only ever learn ten words. Someday the computer should invent aliens stranger than ours. When it does, we won't be upset. We'll be glad, and we'll add the new alien to the test list.

## What we got wrong

We made two mistakes worth telling.

First, we wrote a forever-rule by accident. We said nothing can ever travel faster than light. But a very clever alien people might invent that someday. So we changed "never" to "not yet."

Second, our three aliens are all alike in a sneaky way. All old. All gentle. All easy to like. A checklist we keep caught it. A universe full of only wise, kind grownups has very dull wars. The next aliens will be harder to love.
