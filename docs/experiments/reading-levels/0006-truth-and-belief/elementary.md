# Truth & Belief

*Reading-level experiment · target: elementary school · rewritten from `docs/posts/0006-truth-and-belief.md` · original untouched*

---

We are building a pretend universe inside a computer. You don't play it. You read it, like a storybook that writes itself.

Two characters live in it so far. One is a market, where prices wiggle up and down. One is a war office, which gathers soldiers. For a long time, they ignored each other. The prices wiggled. The soldiers gathered. Neither one noticed the other.

This week, the war office started listening.

Now when prices jump up a lot, the war office gets nervous and gathers eight groups of soldiers. When prices barely move, it calms down and gathers only a few. You can even ask the story, "Why did that happen?" It answers like a detective. The soldiers gathered because of the price jump. The price jump happened because of the day before. And so on, all the way back to the very first moment.

## The big rule

Here is the rule we care about most. The war office is not allowed to see the real world. It can only read its own mail.

Think of it like this. The war office has a mailbox. A mail carrier brings it letters about what happened. When it must decide something, it opens the mailbox and reads. That's it. If no letter about prices ever came, the war office knows nothing about prices. So it does nothing. No letter, no soldiers.

That is how real people work too. You don't act on what is true. You act on what you have *heard*. Sometimes the news is late. Sometimes it's a little wrong, like a game of telephone. We want our pretend civilizations to be like that.

## How we stop cheating

We could just tell the war office, "No peeking!" But rules like that get broken. So we did something better. We never handed it the world at all.

It's like a card game. You can only play the cards in your hand. You can't cheat with a card you were never dealt. The war office is only dealt three things: its mailbox, its own dice, and the number of the day. The world isn't in its hand. So peeking isn't just against the rules. It's impossible.

Right now, the mail carrier is super fast. Letters arrive the same day things happen. Later, we will make mail slow, like news crossing space. The nice part is that the war office won't need to change at all. It was always just reading its mail.

## What we got wrong

We admit our mistakes in every post. Here are two.

First, we found out we had used one name for two different things. That's like a class with two kids named Sam. Confusing! We renamed one of them, and we started a little dictionary so every word in the project means exactly one thing.

Second, we wrote one test that we *plan* to break later. It checks that mail arrives instantly. When we make mail slow, that test will fail on purpose. The failure will be our proof that the news finally learned to travel. A test built to break sounds silly, but it's an honest way to mark something we know is temporary.

## What's next

Next we build two little civilizations. One loves trading. One loves fighting. Each gets its own mailbox. And for the first time, our storybook will show somebody making a trade.
