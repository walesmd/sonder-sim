# The office — a charter

*Card 160. One page, story before theory. Everything named here is
an example the system must be able to support, never a dictation
that it exist.*

## The universe

Bellwether & Co. is a firm of about two dozen people trying to
build a business together. Every employee is an individual mind — a
faction in the engine's sense: a decision-maker with a belief store,
a private chronology, and a place on the map. There is no smaller
unit of history than a person here, which makes this world the
constitution's notable-figures zoom tier, arrived early through the
back door.

The cast is authored as role templates with per-person constants —
temperaments, the Vessari/Khedrun pattern at human scale: a founder
who hears everything late and believes she hears everything first;
managers who relay, filter, and sometimes sit on news; sellers who
live closest to the outside world; makers who turn days into work
product; ops who move the money and know the books better than
anyone believes they do. Twenty-plus people, a handful of minds.

## The map

Distance is social. The org chart and the standing channels are the
map: same team is adjacent, a shared manager is two hops, cross-
department gossip climbs the chart's spine and comes back down, and
a shared channel is a shortcut across all of it. News, documents,
and rumors all travel this graph at the pace the courier already
knows how to enforce. Nothing in the engine changes; only the map's
meaning does. (Someday a reorg is the map moving under everyone's
feet — the moving map arrives here before it arrives in space.)

## The economy

An open system, doors declared: revenue enters when a deal with an
outside company closes; salaries and costs leave on payday. Both
are recorded events — lawful the way the torch is lawful — and the
audit's identity reads: founded + revenue − paid = held + on the
road. One commodity rides the cargo grammar: work product, made by
makers, routed between people (a report making the rounds is
cargo.shipped on the social map, and that is not a metaphor), and
exchanged with clients for revenue. External companies are
environment in v1 — weather with a checkbook — not minds.

## Coalitions

Employees are free to make a coalition that creates a team — and a
team is behavior, not structure: events in belief stores plus minds
acting in concert. Nothing may structurally preclude the team that
exists only in one member's head.

## The war nobody planned

This world's KPI story is the unplanned rumor cascade: something
true happens quietly, its news travels the social map unevenly, and
the company's collective behavior visibly changes before the
company collectively knows why. The acceptance run must produce one
unprompted.

## Stories this world must host (the eval seed)

- The promotion someone hears about from the wrong person, late.
- The team that one member still believes exists.
- The deal that lives entirely in the one seller who knows the
  client — and what the company knows the week she goes quiet.
- The books ops keeps versus the books the founder believes.

## What v1 declines, by name

Hiring and departures (actors joining and leaving mid-history — the
first card this world sends back to the system); clients as
believable minds; meetings with attendee lists (a known stress on
flat payloads, to be met when hit).

## Current state (living; the sections above are the card-160 founding)

Bellwether runs at **rung 1** of the carrier taxonomy: news still
reaches everyone at org-chart pace, but the field is now a row this
world declares (`Carriage.field(1)`) rather than physics the engine
assumes. Its rung 2 — what earshot means in a building: the room,
the thread, the cc line — is card 164, deliberately a design
conversation before it is code. Golden seal `10fc9a5781a44136`
(seed 7 × 200, unmoved since founding). The rumor-cascade KPI
fired unprompted at card 160 and still does.
