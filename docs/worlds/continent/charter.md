# The continent — a charter

*Card 160. One page, story before theory. Everything named here is
an example the system must be able to support, never a dictation
that it exist.*

## The universe

One landmass — call it Harrow until someone earns a better name —
and five civilizations who cannot move away from one another. That
is the whole premise: in space, distance is emptiness; on a
continent, distance is *someone else's territory*. Every road runs
through a neighbor, every border is shared, and geography is
politics before anyone declares anything.

The cast, sketched as examples (fresh minds, authored under the
eval-note practice from day one — nothing ported from the space
shelf):

- **the Valebright** — the grain valley, the continent's granary;
  rich, open, and unwalled, because their wealth *is* their defense:
  everyone needs them fed.
- **the Korrag** — iron in the high country and no fields to speak
  of; proud, hungry every winter, holders of the northern passes.
- **the Selm** — the river people; barges, tolls, and middlemen who
  are everyone's neighbor by water and no one's friend by treaty.
- **the Tethri** — horse people of the eastern steppe; herd-wealth,
  mobile within their range, hard to raid and hard to tax.
- **the Ashfold** — small, positional, keepers of the single
  southern pass; power out of all proportion to their size, and they
  know it.

## The map

An adjacency graph of named regions. Distance is hops through
territory, and the map has *interior*: the Korrag's grain lifeline
runs through Selm tolls or Ashfold's pass; two capitals can be near
as the crow flies and far as the road walks. This is the first map
where distance(a, b) violates naive expectations — around versus
through — which the distance seam was built for and has never yet
had to carry.

## The economy

Two or three commodities with differential needs — grain (the
Valebright have it), iron (the Korrag have it), and salt or horses
as the charter's third, culturally loaded good. **No exchange.**
Trade is bilateral, neighbor to neighbor, struck on each side's
believed prices — which makes information asymmetry tradeable and
middlemen (the Selm) a profession rather than a mechanic. Anything
bilateral trade needs that the engine lacks is a card sent back to
the system, by name.

## Coalitions

Alliances are behavior, not structure — sworn in events, held in
belief stores, and capable of dissolving unevenly: the alliance one
signatory still believes in is a story this world must host.

## The war nobody planned

This world's KPI story is the unplanned border war or famine
crisis: a bad harvest in the valley propagates through bilateral
trade — prices spike, a lifeline through a pass tightens, and a war
starts three borders from the failed field. The acceptance run must
produce one unprompted, with the whole chain legible in cause links
across at least three civilizations' territories.

## Stories this world must host (the eval seed)

- The landlocked civ whose every road is someone else's mood.
- The famine that becomes a war it never touched.
- The middleman who profits from a crisis both sides think he
  caused.
- The pass-keeper who closes the gate once, and what that costs
  everyone forever after.

## What v1 declines, by name

Seasons and harvest cycles beyond the existing daily draw; naval
anything; negotiation mechanics richer than standing intents (a
card if the world proves it needs one); migration and borders that
move (the map stands still until the moving map arrives for every
world at once).

## Current state (living; the sections above are the card-160 founding)

Harrow is the **rung-2 pilot** of the carrier taxonomy (ADR 0005):
the field is retired here. News travels on two declared rows —
*earshot* (loud carries two days; local and quiet stay home) and
*letters* (offers to their buyer, acceptances to their seller) —
and since card 151 the letters carry an encounter profile: one
chance per fifty rider-days, sole outcome loss, recorded
reason-free at `the-roads`, witnessed by no one. The headline
consequence is the half-settled trade — paid, never shipped —
which the charter's settlement-risk clause blessed in advance.
