# The Khedrun

*Hill clans of the toy epoch who keep two ledgers: what grain costs
in cents, and what it costs in patience. When the second ledger
fills, the first stops mattering.*

**Provenance note, unlike every other entry on this shelf:** like
the Vessari, the Khedrun were written engine-first — the martial
half of card 118's crash-test cast, temperament compiled from
constants in `src/worlds/toy.lua` before this page existed. Hosted
since card 118.

## The temperament, as lived

The holds are high, stony, and honest about their arithmetic: six to
eight sacks a day out of the terraces, ten sacks a day into the
clans. The gap is the whole of Khedrun history. It is closed at the
exchange when there is silver and at spearpoint when there is not,
and no Khedrun considers the second method a failure of the first —
both are called *provisioning*, and the same quartermasters plan
both.

Their patience is a real instrument with real graduations. Grain
above one-fifty for seven markets running is an insult. Four hungry
days inside a week is a deeper one — being unable to afford grain
at any price is, to a Khedrun, still being *priced out*, and they
do not starve quietly. When either fuse burns, the war parties ride:
six to twelve to a band, carrying off what they can (a sack per
raider, roughly, and fifty cents of silver per spear), torching some
of what they can't. Ten days of that and the horde goes home
regardless — weariness is also on the instrument — or sooner, if the
granaries feel safe again.

Every declaration names its insults. The clans keep the receipts:
this price, that price, those four hungry days. A Khedrun war has a
bibliography.

## What they know about the Vessari

That the reaches always have grain, that the factors will sell it
right up until the day they won't, and that the silver taken in a
raid comes home lighter each generation — it goes back across the
exchange, bid by bid, buying the same grain it was stolen alongside.
The clans know this. The clans have decided not to know it harder.

## Eval notes

The Khedrun exist to prove the engine can host a martial temperament
whose violence is *economically legible*: war as provisioning, fuses
as patience made mechanical, plunder as circulation. Systems where
war cannot be caused by prices — or where a declaration cannot cite
the exact events that provoked it — fail this entry.
