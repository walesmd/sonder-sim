# The Vessari

*Grain factors of the toy epoch; they believe every price is a
sentence in an argument the market is having with the future, and
that the only sin is selling as if you were afraid.*

**Provenance note, unlike every other entry on this shelf:** the
Vessari were written engine-first. The shelf's other residents are
fiction the engine must grow to host; the Vessari are the crash-test
cast of card 118, temperaments sketched as constants in
`src/worlds/toy.lua` before a word of this page existed. This entry
is the constants, restated as a people — the first lore written *from*
a world instead of toward one. Hosted since card 118; the first
checkmark on the shelf.

## The temperament, as lived

The Vessari hold the reaches — terraced valleys that yield ten to
fourteen sacks on any given day, more than they can eat and less
than they can stop counting. Their culture is warehouse-shaped:
reserve first (fifty sacks, never negotiable, the "winter floor"),
twelve sacks to market on a good day and not one more, because a
seller who floods a market is teaching it to wait him out.

They undercut the posted price by two — always two, a courtesy and a
strategy so old nobody remembers which it started as — but they will
not sell below eighty. Below eighty a Vessari factor closes the
ledger, folds their hands, and waits for the market to remember what
grain is worth. They have waited out sieges this way. They have
waited out *raids* this way, which takes considerably more nerve.

## What they know about the Khedrun

That the holds are hungry, that hungry neighbors with war parties
are a cost of doing business, and that the raids — the granaries
bleeding, the silver carried off, the torched stores — are, when a
factor is being very honest over very strong drink, a kind of
payment schedule. The plunder comes back across the exchange within
the season, one bid at a time. The Vessari have never said the
sentence "the wars are how our customers refinance" out loud. Their
books say it for them.

## Eval notes

The Vessari exist to prove the engine can host a mercantile
temperament: price-taking with a floor, reserve discipline, and the
patience to withhold. Systems that make it impossible to *decline to
sell* — or that force liquidation under pressure — fail this entry.
